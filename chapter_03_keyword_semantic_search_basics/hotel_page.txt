The Marchmont Riverside is a 112-room boutique hotel set on the quiet north bank of the river, a ten-minute walk from the old town and its main square. The building began life in 1904 as a customs house, and the current owners kept the stone facade, the iron staircase, and the tall shuttered windows while rebuilding everything behind them. The result is a hotel that reads as historic from the street and behaves as modern the moment you check in. Rooms run from compact singles under the eaves to river-facing suites with their own balconies, and every one of them was soundproofed during the 2019 renovation, which is the single change guests mention most often in their reviews.

Breakfast is served from seven until half past ten in the vaulted room that was once the customs hall. It is a proper buffet, not a token one: fresh pastries baked on site, a cheese and charcuterie table, eggs cooked to order, strong coffee, and a rotating hot dish that changes with the season. Guests travelling with children are given a smaller side room so the main hall stays calm, a small touch that turns up again and again in family reviews. The kitchen will pack a breakfast box for anyone leaving before seven for an early flight or train, which the front desk arranges the night before.

The location is the hotel's quiet advantage. The main square, the cathedral, and the museum quarter are all within a fifteen-minute walk, and the riverside path outside the front door runs flat and traffic-free in both directions. The nearest metro station is four minutes away on foot, which puts the airport at roughly forty minutes door to door without a taxi. Despite sitting so close to the centre, the street itself is residential and still at night, and the double glazing installed in the renovation means that even the few rooms facing the road stay silent after dark. Several reviewers who booked expecting city noise wrote that they slept better here than at home.

Service is where the Marchmont earns its repeat guests. The front desk is staffed around the clock, the concierge keeps a genuinely useful list of restaurants that locals rather than tourists fill, and the housekeeping team has a reputation for remembering returning guests by name. The hotel is not large enough to feel anonymous and not small enough to feel amateur, which is a balance larger chains rarely manage. When a booking goes wrong, and bookings sometimes do, the staff tend to fix it before the guest has finished explaining the problem, and the reviews reflect that: complaints are rare and almost always answered.

There are trade-offs, and the honest reviews name them. The compact rooms are genuinely compact, and a traveller with a large suitcase and a companion will find the smallest category tight. The hotel has no pool, no gym beyond a single treadmill and a rack of weights in a converted cellar, and no on-site restaurant for dinner, though the concierge will book any of a dozen within a five-minute walk. Parking is limited to eight spaces in the old courtyard and must be reserved in advance, which is a real constraint in a city where street parking near the centre barely exists. The lift is small and slow, a consequence of fitting modern machinery into a 1904 shaft, and at checkout time on a busy morning the queue for it can back up.

Guests consistently rate three things above all others: the quiet, the breakfast, and the location. The quiet is the surprise, because a hotel this close to the old town has no business being this peaceful, and the soundproofing is the reason. The breakfast is the anchor of the morning and worth waking up for even on a travel day. The location puts the whole city within an easy walk while keeping the noise of it at arm's length. Set against those, the small rooms and the limited parking are the price of a historic building in a central spot, and most reviewers decide the trade is worth making. The Marchmont is not the cheapest hotel in the city, nor the most lavish, but for a traveller who values sleep, a good first meal, and a short walk to everything, it is difficult to beat.
